## What it means
1. **HACR_EL2[56] routes the PMUv3 sysreg accesses to EL2 on the M1** [verified: HACR0 readback, `HACR=0x100000000000000` on the panel, CATCH N rising to 1083]. The virtual PMU answers them. bootmgr, the boot.wim stage and the kernel all run past yesterday's stall. That stall (F-42) is solved on hardware.
2. **The PMC0 source works at the guest's EL** once the layer enables counter 0. The panel shows `SRC=PMC0 D=2064792` cycles/ms (~2.06 GHz) [verified]. Since `SRC` reads `PMC0` and not `TIMER(fallback)`, PMC0 moved at the first guest read. `MDCR STUCK=0x60` means TPM|TPMCR did not stick, as F-42 says.
3. **Kernel EL0/EL1 accesses arrive too**: `PMUSERENR_EL0` was caught from a kernel PC.
4. **New stop: bugcheck 0xA0** (F-43). The guest ran with one core started, while the j293 ACPI tables describe eight CPUs. The bench showed the same 0xA0 for the same kind of mismatch (F-37, resolved there by giving the guest all eight cores) [hypothesis for the M1].
5. **Colours** (F-44): the BGR8 firmware reports the 10-bit `x2r10g10b10` panel as 8-bit BGR (E11, untruthful by design), and Windows' pixels come out mis-coloured: the stop screen is red, the logo magenta. The layer's own band has correct colours.
6. **PM0 is NOLOG on the M1** after a forced power-off and a tethered boot: the ring is all zeros, so DRAM is cleared or not retained [hypothesis: which one]. From now on, rely on photos and video only.
